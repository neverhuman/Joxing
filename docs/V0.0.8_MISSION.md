# v0.0.8 mission: distinguish tracking failure from physical failure

Status: ready after v0.0.7 publication; no v8 implementation or outcome yet. One integration owner, no agents. This follows master P1/P2/P3 and the v7 measured bottleneck. The frozen controller stops at its native joint-tracking threshold around 4.1 s; the first registered replay still has foot support, about 10–12 degrees pelvis tilt and no sampled physical failure. That observation motivates a diagnostic, not a claim of long-term balance.

## Objective, scope and completion

Implement physical motor evidence at every physics substep and test whether the same frozen controller remains physically supported after the original reference-tracking failure. Preserve that first native failure time and all v7 conclusions. This explicitly named continuation variant separates two meanings of failure before investing in another motor-training sweep.

Non-goals: relabeling v7 as passed, altering the canonical boxing points/fouls, competitive learning, a stationary-stance/guard claim, modified anatomy, action filtering, reference repairs or new optimization. Walking 20 s would not satisfy quiet stance. Do not lower existing physical or replay gates. Assumptions: the v7 equipment/body-reference adapter and source-host replay are sound; 20 ms support telemetry alone does not establish substep evidence. Dependencies: pinned assets/runtime/archive, v7 actual replay, fresh host capacity and finite valid resets.

Completion means a source-frozen finite experiment with every outcome, source-host replay, independent accounting and a measured decision about the next motor-learning mission. Success threshold proposed for final preregistration:>=16/20 physical 20 s completions per variant, all 40 attempts valid, exact source-host continuation. A negative is a complete diagnostic. Stop on unexplained physics/observation divergence, unknown force ownership, hidden assistance, nonfinite/warnings, missing attempt/state, replay failure or the first resource cap. Never hide a numerical failure as a physical loss.
